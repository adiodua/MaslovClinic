Teeth Whitening: Is It Harmful,
and How to Choose a Safe Method

We unpack the biggest myth about teeth whitening, how in-office whitening differs from at-home whitening, and how long the results last.

Book a Visit
MaslovClinic · Blog

So is teeth whitening actually harmful

Done correctly — no. Modern whitening, such as the Beyond Polus in-office system, works with a light-activated gel that lightens the pigment inside the enamel without breaking down its structure. The discomfort some people feel is temporary tooth sensitivity, not enamel damage, and it fades within a few days.

Who whitening isn't recommended for

The dentist rules out contraindications at a consultation before the procedure: pregnancy and breastfeeding, untreated cavities and gum disease, and significant tooth sensitivity. That's why whitening is usually preceded by an exam, and sometimes by professional hygiene.

In-office vs. at-home whitening — what's the difference

In-office whitening (the Beyond Polus system at our clinic) delivers a noticeable result in a single session under a dentist's supervision. At-home whitening uses custom trays worn for a set time at home as prescribed by the dentist, with results building up gradually. Both options lighten natural enamel by several shades.

How the in-clinic procedure works

Exam and consultation — the dentist assesses the condition of your enamel and gums, rules out contraindications, and selects a target shade. Next comes preparation — protecting the soft tissue and gums, plus professional hygiene if needed. The gel is applied to the enamel and activated with a lamp in several short cycles. The procedure finishes with a desensitizing treatment and a rundown of the "white diet" rules for the first 48 hours.

How long the result lasts, and what it costs

The effect lasts 1 to 2 years if you follow the "white diet" for the first 48 hours and keep up regular hygiene. At MaslovClinic, Beyond Polus whitening starts from 4000 UAH.
