Overview

Pharmacological Relaxation Under Medical Supervision

Sedation is a form of pharmacological relaxation in which the patient stays conscious but feels no anxiety and retains almost no memory of the procedure itself. It is carried out under the supervision of an anesthesiologist.

What It Offers

Relieves anxiety and fear of treatment, reduces the gag reflex, and makes lengthy procedures more comfortable.

Who It's For

Patients with dental phobia, a strong gag reflex, or those undergoing extensive treatment in a single visit.

Safety

Vital signs are monitored throughout treatment by an anesthesiologist.

Visit Steps

How Sedation Treatment Works

  1. Anesthesiologist Consultation

    Assessing contraindications and selecting the appropriate type of sedation before treatment.

  2. Sedation Administration

    Pharmacological relaxation takes effect quickly, and the patient remains conscious.

  3. Treatment Under Supervision

    The dentist performs the necessary procedures while the anesthesiologist monitors the patient's condition.

  4. Recovery

    After treatment, the patient rests under observation for 30–60 minutes before leaving the clinic.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Sedation

Is sedation safe?

Yes, sedation is administered under the supervision of an anesthesiologist, with vital signs monitored throughout the entire treatment.

How is sedation different from general anesthesia?

During sedation, the patient stays conscious and can respond to the dentist's requests, but remains in a deeply relaxed state, free of anxiety, with minimal memory of the procedure.

Who is sedation treatment suitable for?

Patients with pronounced dental anxiety, a strong gag reflex, and those undergoing lengthy or extensive procedures in a single visit.

How much time is needed to recover after sedation?

Usually 30–60 minutes of monitored rest at the clinic is enough, but driving is not recommended on the day of the procedure.
