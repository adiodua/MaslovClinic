What it is

Crown or veneer — what's the difference

A crown fully covers the tooth and suits severely damaged or root-canal-treated teeth. A veneer is a thin facing bonded to the front surface, for the aesthetic smile zone.

Ceramic crown

Fully restores a damaged tooth — suitable after root canal treatment or significant tissue loss.

Ceramic veneer

A thin facing on the front tooth surface — changes shape and color with minimal preparation.

Custom-made for you

Every restoration is crafted from the individual patient's impressions in a dental lab — for a precise fit and shade.

Visit stages

How the procedure works

  1. Examination and planning

    The doctor assesses the tooth's condition, recommends a solution (crown or veneer), and agrees on the shade with you.

  2. Preparation and impressions

    Minimal tooth preparation, followed by impressions or a digital scan for the dental lab.

  3. Temporary restoration

    A temporary restoration is placed while the permanent one is being made.

  4. Fitting and placement

    The finished crown or veneer is tried in, adjusted if needed, and bonded with permanent cement.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about crowns and veneers

How long does a ceramic crown last?

On average 10–15 years or longer with proper care and regular dental checkups.

Does a tooth need to be filed down for a veneer?

Preparation is minimal and often limited to the enamel only — far less than for a crown.

How many visits does a crown or veneer take?

Usually 2–3 visits: preparation and impressions, a fitting, and final placement.

Can teeth with crowns or veneers be whitened?

Ceramic doesn't change color from whitening gels — the restoration's shade is chosen in advance to match your desired smile color.
